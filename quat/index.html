Most study apps start after the hard part

They assume the material is already typed in, already organised, already yours. Quat starts at the lecture.

Audio

Record a lecture, a seminar, or yourself talking through a topic. Quat transcribes it and pulls out what is worth remembering.

Photo

Point your camera at a textbook page, a whiteboard, a handout, or your own handwriting. Quat reads the text off it.

Link

Paste a URL. Quat fetches the page, reads it, and summarises what matters. It reads the actual page, so it cannot invent what is not there.

Review that ends

Spaced repetition schedules each card for the day you are about to forget it. When the day's cards are done, the session ends and tells you so. It does not invent more work to keep you in the app.

No confetti, no streak guilt, no badge that congratulates you for opening the app. Every number is set in a monospaced face with tabular figures, so counts and intervals stay legible at a glance.

Screens

Private by default

No account. No sign-up. Your notes and cards are stored on your device, not on a server.

  • Nothing is uploaded unless you use a capture feature, and then only the thing you handed it
  • No analytics, no tracking, no advertising
  • The App Store product page reads "Data Not Linked to You"
  • Everything except capture works offline

What it costs

Everything that happens on your device — reviewing, exams, games, writing and editing your own notes and cards — is free without limit. A subscription raises the monthly ceilings on the parts that call a model: capture, the tutor, and card generation. $9.99 monthly or $79.99 a year, billed through your App Store account. It renews until cancelled, which you can do at any time in your account settings.
