What "no account" actually gets you, and what it costs

Searching for a study app without a signup is usually two wishes wearing one coat. One is practical: you do not want another password, another verification email, another thing to cancel. The other is about privacy: you do not want your notes becoming somebody's training data or somebody's marketing list.

They are different questions and they have different answers, so it is worth pulling them apart before you choose anything.

No account does not mean nothing leaves your device

This is the confusion worth clearing up first, because plenty of apps are happy to let it stand.

An account is an identity: a row with your email on it that ties your data to you across devices. Removing it removes the identity. It does not, on its own, mean the app is offline. Any app that transcribes audio, reads a photo, summarises a link, or generates cards is sending that material to a model somewhere, because none of that runs on a phone. "No account" and "nothing leaves the device" are two claims, and only the first one is usually being made.

The honest formulation is: no account means nobody can look you up. It does not mean nothing is transmitted. An app that claims both, while offering AI features, is describing something that does not exist.

What to actually check

Read the App Store privacy label, not the marketing page. On any product page, scroll to App Privacy. The categories that matter are "Data Used to Track You" and "Data Linked to You" — those are the ones that require an identity. "Data Not Collected" or "Data Not Linked to You" is a meaningfully different posture, and it is a declaration the developer makes to Apple rather than a sentence they wrote about themselves.

Ask what happens to the material, not just to your identity. If it transcribes lectures, where does the audio go and how long does it stay? A one-line answer in a privacy policy that names the processor is worth more than a paragraph of assurance.

Check whether the AI features have a second door. Capture is the obvious one. A built-in tutor or chat feature is the one people forget, because it usually sends your notes along as context — which means the private thing you wrote is now part of a request. That is not necessarily wrong, but you should know it is happening.

Find out what you lose. This is the part nobody advertises.

The real cost of no account

No account almost always means no sync and no recovery.

If your data lives only on the device, then a lost phone, a broken phone, or an accidental delete takes everything with it, and there is nobody to email about it. There is no "log in on the new one" step, because there is no account for you to log into. This is the actual trade, and it is a real one: the same architecture that means nobody has your data means nobody has your data, including you.

If you go this route, the mitigation is to export periodically. Any app you trust with a term's work should let you get your cards out in a plain format — and if it does not, that is a much bigger warning sign than a signup form.

How Quat handles it

Quat is an iPhone and iPad app I make, so read this as an interested party's description, and check the claims rather than taking them.

There is no account, and there is no account system — no sign-in, no password, no email, no profile on a server. Your notes, cards and review history live in the app on your device. The App Store page reports "Data Not Linked to You", and there is no analytics SDK.

What does leave the device, stated plainly, because a partial version of this sentence was wrong on this site until recently:

  • Anything you capture, when you capture it — the audio you record, the photo you take, the link you paste, the document you add — goes to the service that transcribes or reads it and produces the note.
  • The tutor sends your note text along as context. It is not a capture feature, and it was the omission that made an earlier version of this claim false.
  • A deck you deliberately share leaves the device, sealed with a password that is generated rather than chosen.

Everything else stays put. Reviewing, exams, games and everything already captured work with no network at all.

And the cost, which is the same cost as everywhere else: there is no sync and no backup. Deleting the app deletes your notes and cards, and there is nothing on a server for me to restore, because there is no server holding it. Two devices are two separate sets of notes. If that trade is wrong for you, it is genuinely wrong, and an app with an account is the better answer.

You can export your whole deck as CSV from the Cards tab at any time, which is the mitigation above and the reason it is worth doing before you need it.
